How "me vs the market" is calculated

MIX · YOU (POLICY)

Did you change what you lend to?

change in share × average rate

+49 bps

MARKET · NOT YOU

Did the same borrowers get worse everywhere?

your Feb rate × market's % change × average share

+22.5 bps

OWN · YOU (EXECUTION)

Did your loans do worse than the market's?

(change in rate × average share) − market

+42.5 bps

Backup

Use this only if a judge asks how the split works. Step zero: cut the book into segments of loans that should behave alike, product by state by score band. Mix asks whether you changed what you lend to: for each segment, the change in its share of your book times its average rate. In the example you moved 10% of the book from prime to subprime, which alone added 49 basis points. Market asks whether the same borrowers got worse for every lender: your starting rate in each segment times the market's percentage change in that same segment. Subprime got 10% worse everywhere, so your 6% should have become 6.6%; that's 22.5 basis points across the book, and it isn't your fault. Own is what's left of the rate change: your subprime actually went to 8%, not 6.6%, so the gap is execution, 42.5 basis points. The three parts add to the 114 basis point change exactly, because each uses the average of the two dates. The verdict is "mostly the market" only when the market part is bigger than mix and own combined. In the demo, Gujarat personal loans rose 405 basis points and only 13 came from the market: 61% is the bank's own. Mix and rate are standard attribution any lender can do on its own book; the market leg needs every other lender's rate in the same segment, which only the bureau has. The workbook lets anyone change the inputs and watch the parts move.
